Guide

Five minutes from zero to your first chart. Tap a step to open it. Or jump to a full example.

1Make a nodethe thing you track

Tap the green + at the bottom and give it a name, like 'sleep' or 'bird sightings'.

Nodes can hold other nodes: 'garden' can hold 'tomatoes' and 'basil'. Start broad, add more inside later.

2Add measurementswhat you write down

On the node, tap Configure tracking. Add one field per thing you write down each time, and pick its type: Number, Boolean (yes/no), Text, Timestamp or Category.

A 'morning run' could be Distance (number, km), Felt good (yes/no) and Route (category). Rules are optional, like whole numbers from 1 to 10, which turns the input into tappable numbers.

3Recordone tap to save

Fill in the New observation card and tap Save observation. Each save is one observation, stamped with the time (tap Now ▾ to change it).

Faster: expand a node on the Dashboard and tap +, or answer straight from a notification.

4See it in the Labcharts and patterns

Open the Lab and tap + at the top to start a card. Add measurements to it: one gives a chart, two let the Lab look for a relation.

Tap a measurement on the card for its summary and Data reliability: how sure the app is, or that it needs more data. X axis compares by a category, a weekday or the time of day.

Pin a card to show it on your Dashboard.

5Reminders and random check-inshonest data

Under a node's measurements, Random check-ins asks you at random moments in a time window, so your data shows your whole day, not only the moments you remember.

Fixed reminders are in the node's ⋮ menu → Notifications.

6Goals and calculated valuesoptional

Goals tab: aim for '8 hours of sleep', or simply better than usual, with progress and streaks.

Derivations tab: combine measurements into new ones, like 'pace' from 'distance' and 'time'. They work everywhere a measurement does.

7Your databackup and CSV

Dashboard ⋮ → Save data: a full backup, only the structure, or only the data as CSV for a spreadsheet or Python. Load data merges a backup back in and never overwrites.

Everything stays on your phone, so a backup now and then is a good idea. The app reminds you.

Shortcuts

  • Templates: Dashboard ⋮ → Templates gives ready setups, like 'staying in touch' or 'houseplants'.
  • Tips again: Dashboard ⋮ → Show tips again.
  • Swipe right to remove: a Lab card, a measurement on a card, a dashboard pin. You can undo.

A full example: do cold showers lift your mood?

Six weeks with one hunch, using every part of the app. Open a step to see how.

1Build the treeday 1, two minutes

Make a node 'me' and inside it two nodes: 'mornings' and 'mood'. The tree is just how you like to see it; the Lab can compare anything with anything later.

2Say what you write downmeasurements and rules

'mornings': Cold shower (Boolean), Cold minutes (Number, unit min), Woke up (Timestamp).

'mood': Mood (Number, rule: whole numbers 1–10, so you tap a number instead of typing), Where (Category: Home, Work, Outside), Note (Text).

3Make it effortlessreminders and check-ins

On 'mornings', a fixed reminder at 07:30 on weekdays. Answer it from the notification, or open it and save in two taps.

On 'mood', Random check-ins: about 4 a day between 08 and 22. You're asked at moments you didn't choose, so your mood data isn't only from the times you happened to remember. Answer right in the notification.

4Add a calculated value and a goalderivations and goals

Derivations tab on 'mornings': 'cold minutes a week', the weekly total of Cold minutes. It behaves like any other measurement: chart it, compare it, set goals on it.

Goals tab: Cold minutes at least 10 a week. A ring fills up as the week goes, and a streak starts when you keep it.

5Pin it to the Dashboardyour focus right now

Pin 'mornings' and 'mood'. Expand a card to see the latest values and the goal ring, and tap + to enter an observation without opening the node.

6Week 1: the Lab says waithonest about thin data

Start a Lab card with Mood. The chart is there from day one, but Data reliability says "Not enough to say yet" and names what's missing, like "7 of 20 values". No guessing, no fake trends.

7Week 3: patterns start to showx axis

Set the card's X axis to Where: one bar per place. Maybe Outside is higher. Bars with few values are faded, so a single great day outside can't fool you.

Try Weekday or Hour of day too. The random check-ins make the hours fair.

8Week 6: test the hunchrelations

Add Cold shower to the Mood card. The Lab compares mood on days with and without a cold shower, checks the same day and the next day, and takes out the trend over time first, so "both happened to rise" doesn't count.

You get a plain sentence, like "Mood is higher when Cold shower is yes", with how sure it is (Likely or Clear), or "no clear relation". It says goes with, never causes. Whatever the answer, it's yours.

9Dig deeper, or take the data with youanalysis and CSV

Open Mood on the card and tap More under Analysis: your normal range, whether something changed around a date, weekday and time-of-day patterns, records.

Or Dashboard ⋮ → Save data → only the data, and open the CSV in a spreadsheet or Python. Save a full backup now and then too: everything lives on your phone only.
